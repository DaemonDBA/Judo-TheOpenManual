# Yoko Wakare (Side Separation)

> **Source:** Technique Description and Breakdown sourced from *Kodokan Judo*, pg. 94

---

## Technique Description
> "You break your opponent's balance to his front and sacrifice yourself by dropping on your back and left side. Throw him across your outstretched body."

---

## Technique Breakdown
1. Begin in the right natural posture.
2. Pull your opponent a bit to bring him forward more on his left foot, then break his balance forward and step outside his left foot with your left foot.
3. Bring your right foot forward, too, slide your left foot out to the left, and drop on your left side.
4. Pull down hard with both hands as you fall.
5. Your opponent falls forward over your body and lands at a right angle to you.

---

## Technique Overview

| Category | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hands** | Standard lapel and sleeve grip. When falling, pull with both hands downwards and across your body. |
| **Feet** | Both feet need to cross your opponent's body, putting your fall at a perpendicular angle from them. |
| **Timing** | Opponent must be shifting their weight forward for full effect. |
| **Variations** | There are entry/footwork differences for various angles, but the throw is the same. |

### Combinations
* **As a starting technique:**
  * Yoko Wakare to Ne Waza
* **As a finishing technique:**
  * O Uchi Gari to Yoko Wakare

### Counters
* **If your opponent senses that this technique is coming and drops their weight as you fall, it will be difficult or impossible to successfully land this technique. Their position would then allow them to enter into Ne Waza.

### Transitions to Ne Waza

| Katame Waza (Pins) | Shime Waza (Chokes) | Kansetsu Waza (Joint Locks) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Kesa Gatame | Kata Te Jime | Juji Gatame |
| Tate Shiho Gatame | | Ude Garami |

---

## Tactical / Tournament Usage
This throw is an excellent counter to a failed forward throw (O Goshi, Seoi Nage, etc). While your opponent is still faced forward, drop underneath them and pull across your body.

### Body Type Differences
* **Height:** If your opponent is much taller than you, pay extra attention to the pull when falling. Otherwise, you will lose your grip and the throw will fail.
* **Weight:** If your opponent is heavier and tends to drop their weight lower in defense, you will either need to catch them through setups or consider a different technique.

### Safety
* Once the throw is executed, release your lapel grip, so that the opponent can slap the mat.
